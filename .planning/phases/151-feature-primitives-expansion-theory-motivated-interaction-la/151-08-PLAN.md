<objective>
Screen the tier-1 interaction layer — ROADMAP Phase 151's "Wave 3: interaction IC sweep + Feature
Registry integration" gate — using two methodologies matched to two genuinely different statistical
objects.

Purpose: 21 of the 23 tier-1 features are continuous and go through partial Spearman IC controlling
for their 2 parents. `opex_flag` (~4% sparse) and `quad_witching_flag` (~1% sparse) do NOT.
`docs/research/signal-temporal-atomic-primitives.md` is explicit that Spearman rank correlation on a
near-constant binary is the wrong instrument with degenerate power characteristics — they need an
episode-clustered BCa bootstrap of flag days against matched control days. Running them through the
standard path would produce a technically-computable, statistically-meaningless number.

**Resolution of `151-RESEARCH.md` Open Question 1, decided here:** extend the existing
`alpha.ic.partial_fdr_alpha` pool (migration 206, todo 037) rather than minting a third BH-FDR
family. Rationale: the key and its `feature_ic_scores` columns (`partial_ic`, `partial_ic_p_value`,
`partial_ic_n`, `passes_partial_fdr`) were built for exactly this purpose; the population growing
8 to 21 stays comfortably inside the "50 tests, not 30K" power argument ROADMAP's design rules rest
on; and a third family would mean three BH-FDR populations to reason about with no stated benefit.
Todo 037's original 8-feature cohort is not statistically special — it was a pilot on the same
object.

Output: 1 new APR key, an exclusion filter on the pilot script, a new sparse-flag analysis script,
both sweeps run, and the registry lifecycle outcome recorded.
</objective>
