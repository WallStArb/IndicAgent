## 2. Sign Conventions, Event-Gating Logic & Economic Alignment

### 2.1 The H-A Mathematical Contradiction ([L54–58](file:///home/bg/dev/indicagent/docs/plans/2026-09-06-extreme-volume-divergence-confirmed-reversal-prereg.md#L54-L58))
The document defines the H-A statistic as:
$$\text{extreme\_volume\_divergence}_t = -\text{extreme\_proximity}_t \times \text{volume\_z}_t$$
where $\text{extreme\_proximity}_t = +1$ at lows, and $-1$ at highs.

In [L56–58](file:///home/bg/dev/indicagent/docs/plans/2026-09-06-extreme-volume-divergence-confirmed-reversal-prereg.md#L56-L58), the doc states:
> *"Positive = light volume at a fresh low (bullish-reversal warning) or the equivalent light-volume-at-a-high case (bearish-reversal warning) — the statistic's sign convention is fixed here and not re-derived from the data."*

Let us trace the algebraic truth of this formula:
1. **At a fresh low** ($\text{extreme\_proximity} = +1$):
   - Light volume means $\text{volume\_z} < 0$ (e.g. $-1.5$).
   - Statistic $= -(+1) \times (-1.5) = \mathbf{+1.5}$ (**Positive**).
   - Expected forward return for a bullish reversal is **Positive** ($R > 0$).
   - Correlation: Positive statistic $\longleftrightarrow$ Positive return ($\text{IC} > 0$).
2. **At a fresh high** ($\text{extreme\_proximity} = -1$):
   - Light volume means $\text{volume\_z} < 0$ (e.g. $-1.5$).
   - Statistic $= -(-1) \times (-1.5) = +1 \times (-1.5) = \mathbf{-1.5}$ (**Negative!**).
   - Expected forward return for a bearish reversal is **Negative** ($R < 0$).
   - Correlation: Negative statistic $\longleftrightarrow$ Negative return ($\text{IC} > 0$).

**The Flaw:**  
The formula produces a **negative** number at highs with light volume, directly contradicting the text's assertion that it is positive.  
If an implementer follows the prose description and forces the statistic to be positive at highs ($+1.5$), then at highs, a positive statistic corresponds to negative returns ($R < 0$), while at lows, a positive statistic corresponds to positive returns ($R > 0$). When both extremes are pooled in the single-security panel, the positive and negative correlations will **cancel each other out to zero**.  
Conversely, if the formula $-extreme\_proximity \times volume\_z$ is followed, the statistic is directional (+ for bull, - for bear), but then it implies that **heavy volume at a high** ($\text{volume\_z} = +2.0 \implies \text{stat} = +2.0$) predicts strong upside continuation, and heavy volume at a low ($\text{stat} = -2.0$) predicts downside continuation. This conflates a divergence hypothesis with a momentum/continuation hypothesis.

### 3.1 Fatal Defect: The BY-FDR Gate is Unattainable at $N_{null}=1000$
In [L90–96](file:///home/bg/dev/indicagent/docs/plans/2026-09-06-extreme-volume-divergence-confirmed-reversal-prereg.md#L90-L96) and [L150](file:///home/bg/dev/indicagent/docs/plans/2026-09-06-extreme-volume-divergence-confirmed-reversal-prereg.md#L150), the doc sets $N_{null} = 1000$ for the date-shift null and gates on BY-FDR at $\alpha=0.05$.
- In an empirical permutation/shift test with $N=1000$ resamples, the empirical $p$-value is:
  $$p = \frac{1 + \sum_{b=1}^{N} \mathbb{I}(\text{IC}_{null}^{(b)} \ge \text{IC}_{obs})}{N + 1}$$
  The minimum possible $p$-value (when 0 of 1000 null replicates exceed observed) is $\frac{1}{1001} \approx \mathbf{0.000999}$.
- Under the Benjamini-Yekutieli (BY) procedure for $m$ tests, the sorted $p$-values $p_{(1)} \le p_{(2)} \le \dots \le p_{(m)}$ are compared against:
  $$p_{(k)} \le \frac{k}{m \cdot c(m)} \alpha, \quad \text{where } c(m) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \frac{1}{j}$$
- For the full ETF universe ($m = 231$), $c(231) \approx \ln(231) + \gamma \approx 5.442 + 0.577 = 6.02$.
- The critical value for the top-ranked symbol ($k=1$) is:
  $$p_{(1)} \le \frac{1}{231 \times 6.02} \times 0.05 = \frac{0.05}{1390.6} \approx \mathbf{0.0000359}$$
- For rank $k=10$, the threshold is $\frac{10}{1390.6} \times 0.05 \approx \mathbf{0.00036}$.
- The BY threshold does not reach the minimum discrete resolution of the null ($0.0010$) until rank $k \ge 28$:
  $$k \ge \frac{0.0010 \times 1390.6}{0.05} \approx 27.8 \implies k=28$$
- **Consequence:** Unless at least 28 symbols simultaneously achieve $p = 1/1001$ (zero exceedances out of 1000), **not a single symbol can clear the BY gate**. If 20 symbols have empirical $p = 0.0010$, all 20 are rejected because $0.0010 > \frac{20}{1390.6} \times 0.05 = 0.00072$.  
The doc does not state whether BY-FDR uses asymptotic Fisher-z / Student-t $p$-values or empirical null $p$-values. If empirical, $N_{null}=1000$ breaks the gate.

### 4.1 The Mechanism of the Todo 281 Precedent
In Phase 171, the candidate regime axis was a continuous, rolling statistic:
$$\text{volume\_price} = \text{rolling corr}(|\text{return}|, \text{rel\_volume})$$
evaluated across window lengths $W \in \{60, 120, 250\}$ bars on a continuous time series.
- **What happened:** As $W$ increased from 60 to 250 bars:
  1. Estimation noise dropped, so true signal fraction (correlation across adjacent disjoint windows) rose from $0.18 \to 0.38$.
  2. Rolling smoothing induced strong serial autocorrelation in the feature series.
  3. When fitting a 2-state Gaussian HMM, strong autocorrelation caused likelihood surface ridges and sticky state transitions, causing Baum-Welch EM to diverge across random seeds. HMM identifiability fell from $32/34 \to 27/34$, where noise controls performed better ($30/34$).
- **Key finding:** HMM states failed to separate because of **time-dimension temporal autocorrelation from rolling smoothing**.

### 4.2 Does Checking Multiple Event-Density Thresholds Catch the Same Failure Mode?
**No. It is a completely different failure shape requiring a different test.**

1. **Category Error regarding `build_tiers()` Identifiability:**  
   In [L126–128](file:///home/bg/dev/indicagent/docs/plans/2026-09-06-extreme-volume-divergence-confirmed-reversal-prereg.md#L126-L128) and [L140–144](file:///home/bg/dev/indicagent/docs/plans/2026-09-06-extreme-volume-divergence-confirmed-reversal-prereg.md#L140-L144), the doc mandates:

   If deterministic quantile tiering is used, **identifiability is identically 1.0 everywhere by definition**. Quantile cuts have no random seed, no EM, no local optima, and no likelihood surface. Testing an "identifiability-vs-signal curve" on `build_tiers()` is a mathematical nullity: the identifiability curve is a flat line at 1.0.
2. **Event-Density Thresholds $\ne$ Rolling Estimation Windows:**  
   The doc defines event-density thresholds as *"how close to the extreme counts as 'at' it"* ([L128–129](file:///home/bg/dev/indicagent/docs/plans/2026-09-06-extreme-volume-divergence-confirmed-reversal-prereg.md#L128-L129)). 3. **Disjoint-Window Correlation Breaks on Sparse Events:**  
   Signal fraction in Phase 171 was measured via Pearson correlation across adjacent disjoint temporal windows (e.g. 250 consecutive trading days vs the next 250 days).  
   Sparse event series cannot be correlated across disjoint fixed-time windows because event arrivals are Poisson-like and clustered. In many 60-day or 250-day windows, a symbol will have zero or 2 extreme events. Disjoint-window correlation on sparse, irregular events is ill-posed without an explicit aggregation rule.

### 4.3 The Actual Failure Shape for Sparse Regime Candidates
Turning a sparse event signal into a "regime axis" suffers from two distinct structural failure modes that Track 2 fails to test:
1. **Regime Coverage Void / Sparsity Collapse:**  
   A market regime axis (like `regime_trend` or `regime_volatility`) must assign a valid state to **every bar** (100% coverage). Extreme volume divergence occurs on $< 5\%$ of bars. On the remaining $> 95\%$ of bars, what state does the regime take? If it defaults to 0 or "neutral", the regime is not a regime axis—it is an episodic trade trigger.
2. **IC Separation Monotonicity:**  
   In [L137–138](file:///home/bg/dev/indicagent/docs/plans/2026-09-06-extreme-volume-divergence-confirmed-reversal-prereg.md#L137-L138), Track 2 mentions "IC separation across buckets". For a discrete regime cut to be valid, forward returns or strategy IC must differ monotonically across the quantile tiers (e.g. Tier 1 < Tier 2 < Tier 3), and the between-bucket IC variance must exceed the within-bucket bootstrap variance.

### 5.2 Date-Shift Null on Filtered Sparse Panels ([L90–94](file:///home/bg/dev/indicagent/docs/plans/2026-09-06-extreme-volume-divergence-confirmed-reversal-prereg.md#L90-L94))
- [`_circular_shift_null`](file:///home/bg/dev/indicagent/src/intelligence/statistics/ic_math.py#L180-L200) executes `np.roll(Y, offset)`.
- If $Y$ is the vector of forward returns filtered to extreme events, circularly rolling $Y$ by $k$ matches the feature of extreme event $i$ with the forward return of extreme event $i+k$. That tests whether the feature predicts *which* extreme has a larger return, not whether extremes have predictive power relative to non-extreme periods.
- Conversely, if the shift is done on the calendar date across all bars (dense panel), shifting forward returns by $k$ days will align extreme events on date $D$ with forward returns on date $D+k$ where no extreme occurred. This is the correct null, but it requires a full-panel calendar shift harness, not a simple call to `_circular_shift_null`.

### 5.3 Cross-Sectional Ranking Degeneracy ([L97–99](file:///home/bg/dev/indicagent/docs/plans/2026-09-06-extreme-volume-divergence-confirmed-reversal-prereg.md#L97-L99))
- The doc includes a secondary "Cross-sectional arm: does ranking symbols by `extreme_volume_divergence` / `confirmed_reversal` at the same bar carry a long-short spread".
- On any single bar $t$ during normal market regimes, only a tiny fraction of symbols (often 0, 1, or 2 out of 231) are printing a 20-bar rolling low or high.
- Ranking 231 symbols where 229 symbols have a value of `0` (or NaN) results in a completely degenerate cross section where quintile spreads cannot be formed. Cross-sectional ranking is only mathematically viable on days with broad market-wide extremes (e.g. crash days), creating massive survivorship and event-clustering distortions.
