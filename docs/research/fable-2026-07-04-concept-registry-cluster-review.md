# Concept Registry Cluster Review - The Hub Is Behind Its Own Consumers

**Date:** 2026-07-04 · **Author:** Fable 5 (dispatched via Claude Code Agent tool) · **Type:** research/review, read-only
**Scope:** cross-document review of `docs/research/concept-governance-registries.md` (the hub) against its consumers and siblings - `intel-10` v3, `intel-13`, `intel-14`, `intel-15`, `intel-12`, `feature-registry.md`, `interaction-factory.md`, `controlled-vocabulary.md` - plus ROADMAP.md (post-2026-07-04 renumbering), the pending-todo set, and the two predecessor Fable reviews (2026-07-03 intel10-11, canonical-simulator). Verified against live DB state where claims were checkable.
**Verdict up front:** the hub's design substance holds - the four-table MVP discipline, the gate-proves/annotation-explains invariant, the six self-improvement invariants, and the APR-not-a-peer taxonomy all survive scrutiny. What has broken is *synchronization*: the hub's own build trigger fired (142B.1 ✅ 2026-07-04) and no artifact tracks the next step; two of the first three real domains (`ensemble_strategy`, `regime_model`) have per-stratum status requirements the MVP schema cannot represent as written; the hub's re-evaluation invariant is statistically weaker than the standard its own consumer (intel-14) already adopted for the identical problem; and consumer docs have silently settled at least three questions the hub still records as open. Findings F1-F3 need action before the MVP is built; the rest are doc-sync debt.

ROADMAP.md Phase 142B.1 is ✅ COMPLETE (2026-07-04), and its own closing note is explicit: *"Next step once 142B.1 completes: a small follow-on item seeding `concept-governance-registries.md`'s four-table MVP from 142B.1's E1-E4 `weight_version` rows... Meanwhile the hub doc still speaks in future tense - *"the only upstream dependency is Phase 142A completing - a phase already in flight"* and *"if/when the MVP build trigger fires"* - i.e., the hub does not know its own trigger condition is now true. The exact failure mode the registry exists to prevent (an earned decision evaporating into nobody's queue) is happening to the registry itself.

**Fix:** create the seeding todo (full spec in the punch list - it must incorporate F2/F3/F7/F8 below, which is why this review specifies its content rather than just its existence), and update the hub's build-trigger paragraph to record the trigger as fired.

### F2. Both of the first two real domains need per-stratum status, and the MVP's single global `status` column cannot represent either - this must be decided before the first migration is written. [HIGH]

The hub's own instruction on domain mapping is *"decide against that domain's real needs, not by assertion here."* Applying it:

- **`ensemble_strategy`:** 142B.1-05's shipped judge (`ops_ensemble_weight_compare.py`) selects a champion **per (tf, regime) stratum** (D-10..D-14). E2 can win `high_bear/1h` while E1 holds everything else. One global `status` per concept, plus `redundancy_group`'s rule that *"only one holds `active`,"* cannot express two strategies simultaneously legitimate in different strata.
- **`regime_model`:** intel-12's Governance section states outright: *"`concept_registry` records status per (dimension, regime_group); a dimension live for `equity` and shadow for `rates` is a normal state, not an exception."* The schema intel-12 is describing does not exist - `concept_registry.status` is one column per row, and neither the MVP nor the ten-table reference architecture has scoped status.

So a consumer doc is already promising a capability the hub never designed, and the first domain to be seeded hits the same wall from a different direction. The cleanest resolution uses the hub's own concepts-vs-facts boundary: **`status` governs recipe validity** ("has this strategy/dimension earned legitimacy anywhere, under what gate"), while **per-stratum deployment is a fact**, living where deployment facts already live (`ensemble_weights` champion `weight_version` per stratum; conditioning config per `regime_group`). Under that reading, `redundancy_group`'s displacement rule must get a domain-level opt-out for `ensemble_strategy` (competing weighting strategies are the *normal* state, resolved per stratum by the A/B judge, not by registry displacement). The alternative - one concept row per (dimension, regime_group), preserving single-status semantics and per-scope transition logs - fits intel-12's audit-trail intent better for `regime_model`. These may legitimately resolve differently per domain; what is not acceptable is building the MVP without deciding, because the very first `concept_transition_log` rows encode the choice.

**Fix:** operator decision at seeding time (punch list); hub gains a note at `redundancy_group`/`status` recording the tension so the schema isn't built blind; intel-12's "records status per (dimension, regime_group)" gets qualified to whichever mechanism is chosen.

### F3. Invariant 2 ("re-evaluation must consume new evidence") is enforced by a check the cluster itself has already proven insufficient one level down. [HIGH]

Hub invariant 2 closes the self-inflicted look-elsewhere effect with: *"a concept cannot be evaluated twice against the same corpus build... even just a monotonic build identifier compared against the log's last row."* But the hub also says corpus rebuilds happen *"every few days,"* and intel-14 - adjudicating the identical question for feature recovery - already rejected corpus-advance as an evidence criterion: *"Consecutive corpus runs are not independent evidence - rebuilds run days apart on mostly-overlapping windows, so two passes can double-count the same fluke,"* adopting a floor of ≥ `alpha.ic.decay_recovery_min_observations` (2000) **new independent observations** instead (now canon in ROADMAP Phase 143 LIFECYCLE-01). An AI proposer resubmitting a borderline candidate every rebuild is re-rolling ~95%-correlated dice; invariant 2 as written slows the p-hack, it does not close it. This is a silent-wrong-answer risk at the exact mechanism the self-improvement section calls load-bearing ("need to be right from the first line of code").

**Fix:** restate invariant 2 as evidence-mass denominated - re-evaluation permitted only when ≥ N new independent observations (per-domain, a `concept_gate` field, same key family as the recovery floor) have accrued since the concept's last evaluation, with corpus-advance kept as the cheap necessary-but-not-sufficient precondition. One schema consequence for the MVP: `concept_transition_log` needs a `corpus_build_ref` (CorpusManifest identity) column so the comparison has something to compare against - the reference architecture parks this on `concept_eval_run`, which the MVP correctly omits, but omitting the *column* too leaves invariant 2 enforcing against `triggered_at` timestamps, which is weaker than the hub's own intent.

### F4. Confirmed: the `decaying` mapping is settled in intel-10 v3 but still hedged in the hub - and the predecessor review's "no drift" verdict is what created the drift. [MEDIUM]

Verified against live text. Hub (MVP build-trigger section): *"...which suggests the enum survives unchanged and `decaying` is a transition pattern, not a new state - but decide against that domain's real needs, not by assertion here."* intel-10 v3 (Governance): *"`decaying` maps onto the registry's status enum as a transition pattern (`active → shadow_only` re-entry), not a new status - see that doc's mapping note."* The consumer states as settled what the hub still offers as tentative. Note the mechanism: the 2026-07-03 review's F4 verified v2-vs-hub as "no drift, keep exactly as written," then its R1 rewrite hardened the mapping into settled fact - correctly, per that review's own sharpening ("a confluence concept row is a `concept_registry` row whose gate stack is gates 1-6") - without back-propagating to the hub. The settlement is right; only the hub's hedge is stale.

One residue inside intel-10 v3 itself: its "Decay Is the Steady State" section still narrates `active → decaying` and `→ retired` as if they were statuses, and `retired` is mapped nowhere (it can only be `deprecated`). Harmless as domain shorthand, but an implementer reading that section alone would add two enum values the Governance section forbids.

**Fix:** hub replaces the hedge with the settled statement plus provenance; intel-10 adds one line mapping `retired → deprecated` (or restates the decay section in enum terms with the domain vocabulary marked as shorthand).

### F5. Three more hub-recorded open items have been silently resolved (or invalidated) by consumers without the hub being told. [MEDIUM]

1. **The evaluation engine (hub refinement item 15: "load-bearing and unspecified... likely a `BaseBatch` oneshot, but that's a guess").** intel-14 has since decided this for `domain='feature'`: *no* daemon and *no* module - the transition writer is a post-run hook in `ic_engine` calling the narrowly-scoped registry service method, explicitly citing *"concept-governance's invariant 1"* as the shaping constraint; ROADMAP Phase 143 adopted it 2026-07-03. And for `ensemble_strategy`, the de facto engine already shipped: `ops_ensemble_weight_compare.py`'s win-decision gate is the deterministic code path invariant 1 requires. Item 15's gap is now two-thirds closed and the hub still records it as fully open - worse, its "BaseBatch oneshot" guess points the eventual builder away from the pattern intel-14 already reasoned to (status can only change when new measurements land, so the writer belongs at the end of the measurement run, not on its own timer).
2. **The confluence gate-template row.** The 07-03 review's Open Question 3 leaned *"add the row to the doc's Domains table now, build nothing."* Never executed: the Domains table has no `confluence` row, and `confluence` is absent from the `domain` CHECK list even though intel-10 v3, ROADMAP (142B.1 note: *"before any `confluence`/`regime_model` domain needs it"*), and the v3.15 milestone text all use it as a domain name. Today the hub's CHECK constraint and its consumers disagree about the domain vocabulary.
3. **The embedding domain.** intel-13 OQ6 asserts *"one `concept_registry` row per `embedding_version` per D9,"* and todo 055 plans Phase 148's `embedding_feature_registry` becoming `concept_registry` rows. An embedding recipe (feature set, normalization, ordering) is none of the hub's seven domains. A second consumer is claiming registry residency in a domain that doesn't exist.

**Fix:** hub item 15 records both resolutions; Domains table gains a `confluence` row (gate template = intel-10's gates 1-6, marked anticipated) and an anticipated-domains note covering the embedding/analog-substrate recipe (name it at v3.2 planning - `embedding_spec` or fold under a widened `feature` reading, but decide in the hub, not implicitly in intel-13). Keep the CHECK constraint at 7 values until a domain has candidates; the point is that the *hub* lists what's coming so CHECK-vs-consumer drift stays visible.

### F6. `alpha_pattern` is drifting toward a vestigial, ambiguous domain - exactly the bucket an autonomous proposer would fill with junk. [MEDIUM]

The Domains table defines `alpha_pattern` as *"alpha signal ideas competing for ensemble inclusion."* Post one-model-one-book (now in `principles.md`) and intel-10's confluence-as-predictor rewrite, *everything* competing for ensemble inclusion is a predictor, and every concrete predictor family now has a crisper home: dense deterministic transforms → `feature_interaction`; sparse conditional predictors carrying a calibrated return distribution, NULL where the condition is absent → `confluence`; retrieval-derived columns → intel-13's predictors at feature grain. The hub's own text half-acknowledges the overlap ("`confluence`, or `alpha_pattern` if merged"). No two docs currently claim the same domain outright - but only because `alpha_pattern` has no candidates; its definition is a superset of two other domains' definitions, and the self-improvement section names it the primary AI-proposer domain. An ambiguous catch-all is the worst possible bucket to hand an autonomous proposer under invariant 3's volume budget: the budget caps count, not category confusion.

**Fix:** either give `alpha_pattern` a residual definition that excludes its siblings (e.g. emission-rule recipes - thresholded firing logic over predictors, distinct from the predictors themselves - if that's ever a governed thing) or mark the row *reserved pending definition; proposals routed to `confluence`/`feature_interaction` until defined*. No schema change; one table edit.

### F7. The seeding instruction's raw material is misdescribed - only `weight_version='v1'` exists; seed recipes, not rows, and seed the gate into `concept_gate`, not `concept_gate_template`. [MEDIUM]

Verified in DB 2026-07-04: `ensemble_weights` contains exactly one `weight_version` (`v1`, 103 rows). E1/E2 shipped as *code paths* (`shrinkage.py`, `mean_variance_weights()`, APR-toggled `ic_input`/`weight_method`, `weight_version` a run label from `alpha.ensemble.weight_version` or CLI); E3/E4 were deferred at decision level and have neither code nor rows. So ROADMAP's *"seeding... from 142B.1's E1-E4 `weight_version` rows"* overstates twice: there are no E1-E4 rows, and two of the four candidates exist only as theses. The correct seed set: `ic_proportional`/v1 as the `active` incumbent (with its transition-log genesis row), E1 and E2 as evaluated-mechanism candidates, E3/E4 as `candidate` rows with `thesis` annotations (`source='human'`) and no eval. Separately, the hub's Domains-table sentence *"this table is now literally the seed data for that table [concept_gate_template]"* targets a reference-architecture table the MVP deliberately excludes - under the MVP the seed lands as per-concept `concept_gate` rows. Small wording, but it's precisely the kind of instruction a mechanical executor follows into building the wrong table. The ‡ footnote also needs its tense fixed ("informal practice today" / "if/when... reaches its MVP build trigger") and should cite the shipped judge by name.

### F8. The hub's mandatory `baseline_metric` shrinkage has no mechanism at ensemble grain - the first seeded domain would violate the hub's own rule on day one. [MEDIUM]

The hub requires `baseline_metric` store the *shrunk* estimate at promotion ("applies equally to the MVP's `concept_gate` last-eval cache columns") because the promotion gate selects on the metric, inflating the raw promotion-time value. intel-15 OQ7 establishes the same bias exists at ensemble grain - champion selection per stratum among E1-E4 variants is itself a search, so the winner's measured `alpha_ensemble_ic` is upward-biased - and confirms *"nothing in 142B.1's plans addresses this."* 142B.1 shipped `shrink_ic` at **feature** grain only. Left unresolved, the first `ensemble_strategy` promotion writes a raw selected-on metric into `baseline_metric`, and `decay_ratio` demotions later fire on regression-to-the-mean - the exact zombie-killing-healthy-concepts failure the shrinkage note exists to prevent. The hub already names the adequate interim: *"minimally, the mean of the `min_promotion_consecutive` evaluations rather than the final one."* That costs nothing and must be in the seeding todo; proper ensemble-grain shrinkage stays parked at intel-15 OQ7 (which correctly notes it belongs in the shared kernel if built).

APR governs the registry's knobs; the registry governs concepts. No change needed.

Three consumers, zero duplicate specs - the cluster's best example of the discipline working.
